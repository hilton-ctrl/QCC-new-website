## PART A — The Market & Avatar

**1. Who exactly is the buyer?**
Two buyers, one primary:
- **Primary — "Debra," 38–55, Gold Coast homeowner.** Two kids and/or a dog, dual income ~$110–140k, house-proud, time-poor. Books for pets, smells, stains, guests, and "the house feels grubby."
- **Secondary — "Renter Rachel," 25–40, end-of-lease tenant.** $2,000–3,500 bond on the line, inspection date fixed, property manager watching. Highest urgency, most fear-driven.

The review evidence supports both segments strongly (bond/inspection triggers are Tier 1; pets are Tier 2).

**2. Trigger event.**
- Renter: lease ending, inspection booked, property manager has flagged carpets. Hard deadline.
- Homeowner: pet accident that won't stop smelling; guests/family event coming; the sun hits the floor and the stains/traffic lanes are suddenly undeniable.
Review phrases: "got my bond back," "saved us," "removed stains I didn't think would budge."

**3. Dominant Resident Emotion (DRE).**
**Anxiety about being judged + fear of loss.** For renters it's concrete ($ bond, landlord rejection). For homeowners it's social ("I don't want people thinking my house is dirty," "nobody should notice the smell"). Underneath both sits a second fear the reviews surface constantly: **fear of being ripped off by the cleaner themselves** — hidden charges, bait-and-switch, no-shows, upsells. The prospect is anxious about the carpet AND anxious about hiring you.

**4. Identity they want.**
"The responsible, put-together homeowner/tenant." Good parent, good pet owner, financially competent, in control. A clean carpet = a reset, a fresh start.

## PART B — Pains & Desires

**5. Top pains, in their words.**
1. "Stains still visible after cleaning" / "pet smells came back" → *"was that money wasted? Is this permanent? Will I have to replace the carpet?"*
2. "Hidden charges / the price changed when they arrived / felt pressured into upsells" → *"I got ripped off."*
3. "They were late / cancelled / never followed up" → *"I can't rely on anyone; I have to chase them."*

**6. Core desire — end state.**
Walk into a home that looks, feels and **smells** like new again — and know for certain the job was done properly, at the price quoted, by someone who cared. (The report's own synthesis: they're buying relief, pride, peace of mind, and protection from judgment/financial loss — not carpet cleaning.)

**7. Dominant primal driver.**
**Avoid-loss + pride-of-home.** (Bond money, carpet-replacement cost, social judgment.) Results-without-work is secondary.

## PART C — What They've Tried & What They Believe

**8. What they've tried that failed.**
Cheap $99-special operators; supermarket sprays; hired Bunnings-style machines; a previous "professional" who left stains, wet carpet, or a surprise bill.

**9. WHY it failed (the real problem).**
The reviews point at **the operator, not the method**: rushed jobs, no attention to detail, quotes not honoured, wrong treatment for the stain type, over-wetting that leaves carpet damp for days and lets smells return. The real problem the prospect can't solve alone: *from the outside, every carpet cleaner looks identical — you can't tell a careful owner-operator from a cowboy until it's too late.*

> **⚠ VSL CONFLICT #1 — the mechanism-of-the-problem.** The old VSL blames "buried Broadwater sand + wet steam cleaning" as the villain and sells a "Tri-Active Dry-Extraction Protocol" as the fix. The review data does **not** support this. Customers rarely mention machinery at all (the report calls this out explicitly: "People rarely mention machinery. They constantly mention trust."). The evidenced fears about wetness are real but narrow (carpets staying wet too long, mould) — they don't add up to "steam cleaning is the enemy." Worse, QCC's own site assets show hot-water-extraction wands, so an anti-steam-cleaning pitch would attack the client's actual method. The enemy the reviews hand us is the **cowboy operator** (hidden charges, bait-and-switch, rushed work, no-shows), not steam.

**10. The enemy.**
Cheap bait-and-switch operators: the $99-special crowd who quote low, arrive late (or not at all), rush the job, add charges on the spot, soak the carpet, and vanish when the stains return.

**11. Existing beliefs (belief map).**
- "All carpet cleaners are the same" → **REPLACE** (with the trust/process proof).
- "The stains are probably permanent / carpet's too old" → **REFRAME** (right treatment for the stain type; before/afters).
- "They'll upsell me / the quote will change" → **REPLACE** (fixed upfront pricing, no surprises).
- "I could DIY with a hired machine" → **REFRAME** (DIY machines over-wet and under-extract; fine for maintenance, not for stains/odour/bond).
- "Wet carpet for days = mould and smell" → **REINFORCE** (fast-dry process; this belief works for us).
- "Professional cleaning matters for the bond/inspection" → **REINFORCE**.

## PART D — Market Diagnosis

**12. Sophistication: Level 3–4.** Every Gold Coast competitor claims "deep clean, stain removal, satisfaction guaranteed." A named Unique Mechanism is mandatory — but per the review data it must be built on **trust and process transparency**, not on machinery claims.

**13. Awareness: Solution-Aware.** They know carpet cleaners exist; many have used one and been burned. → **Problem-Solution lead** built around "why the last clean didn't last / why quotes can't be trusted," not a pure problem lead.

## PART E — Solution & Mechanism

**14. Solution, plainly.** Owner-operated professional carpet, upholstery and pet-odour cleaning by Mike & Jack — the two people who quote the job are the two people who do it.

**15. What's genuinely NEW (the new opportunity).**
A clean where **nothing is left to chance or trust-me pricing**: the price is locked before the van arrives, the owners themselves do the work, and the job isn't finished until you've walked the rooms and signed off. That's what the reviews say people are actually shopping for and can't find.

**16. The mechanism (named).**
**The Walk-Through Guarantee Clean** *(working name — alternatives: "The Locked-Quote Deep Clean," "The Mike & Jack Sign-Off System")*. Three parts, each aimed at a top-3 review fear:
1. **Locked Quote** — itemised price confirmed before the day; the number on the quote is the number on the invoice. *(kills hidden charges/bait-and-switch)*
2. **Owner-Done, Stain-Matched Clean** — Mike or Jack personally assesses each stain type and treats it with the matched process (enzyme for pet, extraction for soil, specialist spotters for wine/rust), with controlled moisture so carpets are walkable the same day. *(kills "rushed job / wrong treatment / wet for days")*
3. **The Walk-Through Sign-Off** — you inspect every room with us before we leave; we don't pack the van until you're happy. *(kills "poor results / no accountability / had to chase them")*

> **⚠ VSL CONFLICT #2 — mechanism identity.** This replaces the VSL's "Tri-Active Dry-Extraction Protocol." The old mechanism was ownable-sounding but (a) not supported by voice-of-customer evidence, (b) potentially factually wrong about QCC's method, and (c) fragile — a prospect who Googles "dry carpet cleaning vs steam" finds industry consensus favouring hot water extraction for deep cleans, which would detonate credibility.

**17. Google-trap test.** "Walk-through sign-off + locked quote + owner-operated" can't be Googled into a cheaper generic version — it's a business practice, not a chemical. Passes, provided we name it and describe the three steps specifically. ❓CLIENT: confirm Mike & Jack will actually honour all three steps operationally.

**20. Price + value frame.** ❓CLIENT for real numbers. Anchor against **avoid-loss numbers the reviews validate**: a $2,000–3,500 bond, or $3,000+ to re-carpet a lounge. (The VSL's $550→$297 frame is a reasonable shape but the figures were invented.)

**21. Bonuses.** Candidates (❓CLIENT): fabric-protection/stain-shield application; free doormat-zone or hallway refresh; priority re-spot visit within 3 months. Keep to what Mike & Jack will genuinely deliver.

**22. Risk reversal.**
- Homeowner: "If a treated stain reappears within 14 days, we come back and re-treat it free." ❓CLIENT
- End-of-lease: "If the property manager flags the carpets, we re-clean the flagged areas free." ❓CLIENT — **per project rules, no 'bond back guaranteed' wording without client confirmation.** The re-clean promise is the compliant version.

**23. Urgency (real only).** Genuine calendar capacity ("X slots left this week" only if true), end-of-lease deadlines (inherent, real), pre-holiday/inspection seasonality. No fake timers; no "12 homes a week" unless verified.
